# Climate Factors Knowledge Base

## Overview
Climate strongly influences soil processes, water availability, crop viability, species survival, and ecosystem function. Key climate variables for biodiversity decision-making include rainfall, temperature, drought frequency, and seasonal variability.

## Key Climate Variables
1. Rainfall
   - Influences water availability, biomass production, and habitat quality.
   - Low or highly variable rainfall can reduce vegetation cover and species survival.

2. Temperature
   - Affects growing season, evapotranspiration, microbial activity, and species distribution.
   - Extreme heat can stress crops, soil biota, and wildlife.

3. Drought Frequency
   - Repeated drought can reduce plant cover, weaken soils, and reduce habitat suitability.

4. Seasonal Variability
   - Shifts in rainfall and temperature seasonality affect breeding, pollination, and ecosystem timing.

5. Extreme Events
   - Floods, heat waves, and storms can rapidly damage ecosystems and agricultural landscapes.

## Climate Risks to Biodiversity
- Low rainfall can reduce plant cover and habitat availability
- High temperature can increase evaporation and stress organisms
- Repeated drought can reduce soil carbon and biological activity
- Flooding can cause erosion and nutrient loss
- Climate instability can disrupt flowering, pollination, and migration cycles

## Climate-Smart Interventions
1. Mulching and Residue Cover
   - Reduces evaporation and protects soil temperature.

2. Agroforestry and Tree Cover
   - Creates microclimates, reduces heat stress, and improves moisture retention.

3. Water Harvesting
   - Farm ponds, contour bunds, and infiltration structures improve drought resilience.

4. Drought-Resilient Cropping Systems
   - Crop diversification and resilient varieties reduce climate risk.

5. Landscape Heterogeneity
   - Mixed habitats improve resilience to climate variability.

## Climate and Recommendation Logic
- Low rainfall + low soil carbon → prioritize water retention and cover crops
- High temperature + low tree cover → prioritize agroforestry and shade structures
- Drought-prone + monoculture → diversify crops and improve soil organic matter

## Impacted Metrics
- Soil moisture
- Vegetation cover
- Species survival
- Carbon retention
- Drought resilience

## Time Horizons
- Short term: reduced evaporation and better water use efficiency
- Medium term: improved moisture storage and vegetation recovery
- Long term: stronger ecosystem resilience and climate adaptation
