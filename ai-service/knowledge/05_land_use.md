# Land Use and Human Impact Knowledge Base

## Overview
Land use and land cover strongly influence biodiversity outcomes. The way land is managed determines habitat quality, fragmentation, runoff, erosion, pollution exposure, and ecosystem connectivity.

## Important Land Use Categories
- Forest
- Agricultural monoculture
- Mixed farming systems
- Grassland
- Wetland
- Urban or peri-urban land
- Degraded or bare land

## Why Land Use Matters
1. Habitat Fragmentation
   - Breaking natural habitats into smaller patches reduces species movement and breeding success.

2. Vegetation Cover
   - More permanent and diverse vegetation usually supports more biodiversity.

3. Edge Effects
   - Fragmented landscapes increase exposure to heat, wind, and disturbance.

4. Pollution Pressure
   - Urban runoff, chemical inputs, and waste reduce ecological quality.

5. Deforestation
   - Removes habitat, reduces carbon storage, and disrupts water cycles.

## Common Human Pressures
- Deforestation
- Pesticide overuse
- Fertilizer runoff
- Industrial pollution
- Unsustainable grazing
- Urban sprawl
- Water extraction

## Land Use Improvement Strategies
1. Reduce Fragmentation
   - Maintain connected habitat patches and ecological corridors.

2. Add Buffer Zones
   - Riparian buffers and vegetation strips reduce runoff and provide habitat.

3. Restore Degraded Land
   - Use native vegetation, controlled grazing, and erosion control.

4. Reduce Pollution Load
   - Better nutrient management, less over-application of chemicals, and improved waste control.

5. Mixed Land Systems
   - Integrating crops, trees, and natural vegetation usually supports more ecological functions than uniform land cover.

## Recommendation Patterns
- Deforestation + species decline → habitat restoration and corridor creation
- Monoculture + low habitat diversity → introduce mixed land elements and margins
- Pollution + declining aquatic or terrestrial biodiversity → reduce chemical load and install buffer strips
- Bare land + erosion → restore cover with native grasses, shrubs, or trees

## Impacted Metrics
- Habitat diversity
- Species richness
- Water quality
- Soil erosion risk
- Carbon storage
- Landscape connectivity

## Time Horizons
- Short term: reduced runoff and better cover
- Medium term: improved habitat quality and soil condition
- Long term: biodiversity recovery and more stable ecosystem functioning
