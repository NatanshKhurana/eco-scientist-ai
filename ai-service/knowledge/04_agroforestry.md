# Agroforestry Knowledge Base

## Overview
Agroforestry is the intentional integration of trees and shrubs with crops and or livestock. It is widely recognized as a strategy that can improve biodiversity, soil health, water regulation, and climate resilience.

## Why Agroforestry Works
1. Increases Habitat Complexity
   - Trees create vertical structure and habitat diversity.
   - Supports birds, insects, pollinators, and soil organisms.

2. Improves Soil Organic Matter
   - Leaf litter and root turnover contribute organic material.
   - Helps improve soil aggregation and biological activity.

3. Reduces Erosion
   - Tree roots stabilize soil and reduce runoff.

4. Improves Water Regulation
   - Can increase infiltration and reduce moisture stress in some systems.

5. Provides Ecological Connectivity
   - Tree strips and boundary planting connect fragmented habitats.

## Common Agroforestry Models
- Boundary tree planting
- Alley cropping
- Silvopasture
- Windbreaks and shelterbelts
- Home gardens
- Mixed tree-crop systems

## When to Recommend Agroforestry
- Low biodiversity on monoculture farms
- Fragmented habitats
- Heat stress and low rainfall
- Soil erosion problems
- Need for long-term resilience and diversified output

## Cautions
- Tree species selection matters
- Poorly selected species may compete for water or light
- Local ecological suitability must be considered
- Benefits vary by climate, species, spacing, and management

## Agroforestry Benefits by Metric
- Soil organic carbon: may improve over time
- Habitat diversity: increases strongly
- Pollinator support: often improves with flowering and shelter
- Water infiltration: may improve depending on design
- Climate resilience: generally improves through diversity and buffering

## Recommendation Patterns
- Monoculture wheat + low biodiversity + low carbon → agroforestry with legumes and local trees
- Hot region + low tree cover → shade-providing tree integration
- Erosion-prone land → windbreaks, contour trees, and perennial strips

## Time Horizons
- Short term: microclimate and habitat benefits begin
- Medium term: soil and biodiversity improvements become more visible
- Long term: stronger ecosystem services and landscape resilience
