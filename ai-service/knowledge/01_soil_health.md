# Soil Health Knowledge Base

## Overview
Soil health refers to the continued capacity of soil to function as a living ecosystem that sustains plants, animals, and humans. Healthy soil supports crop productivity, regulates water, stores carbon, cycles nutrients, and provides habitat for soil organisms.

## Key Soil Health Indicators
1. Soil pH
   - Controls nutrient availability and microbial activity.
   - Most agricultural crops perform best around pH 6.0 to 7.5.
   - Strongly acidic or alkaline soils can reduce nutrient uptake and biological activity.

2. Soil Organic Carbon (SOC)
   - SOC is a major indicator of soil fertility and biological activity.
   - Low SOC often indicates degraded soil, poor aggregation, and low microbial abundance.
   - Increasing SOC generally improves soil structure, nutrient cycling, and water-holding capacity.

3. Soil Moisture
   - Soil moisture affects plant growth, microbial life, and decomposition.
   - Very low moisture reduces biological activity and crop resilience.
   - Excess water can reduce oxygen and stress roots and soil fauna.

4. Soil Structure and Compaction
   - Good soil structure improves root penetration, aeration, and infiltration.
   - Compacted soils limit water movement, reduce rooting depth, and suppress soil biodiversity.

## Common Signs of Soil Degradation
- Low organic matter
- Surface crusting
- Poor infiltration
- Erosion
- Reduced earthworm activity
- Declining crop yields
- Increased dependence on synthetic inputs

## Practices to Improve Soil Health
1. Cover Crops
   - Protect soil from erosion.
   - Improve organic matter.
   - Support microbial diversity.
   - Legume cover crops may improve nitrogen availability.

2. Compost and Organic Amendments
   - Add stable organic matter.
   - Improve microbial activity.
   - Increase water retention and nutrient holding capacity.

3. Reduced Tillage or Conservation Tillage
   - Helps preserve soil structure.
   - Reduces disturbance to soil organisms.
   - Supports carbon retention over time.

4. Residue Retention
   - Leaving crop residues on the field protects the surface, reduces evaporation, and contributes organic matter.

5. Crop Rotation
   - Diverse rotations interrupt pest cycles and improve nutrient cycling.
   - Deep-rooted and legume crops can help restore soil condition.

6. pH Management
   - Acidic soils may require liming.
   - Alkaline or sodic soils may require gypsum or other corrective management depending on conditions.

## Soil Health and Biodiversity Link
Healthy soils support bacteria, fungi, earthworms, nematodes, arthropods, and plant roots. Increased soil biological activity often leads to better nutrient cycling, better plant vigor, and greater above-ground biodiversity such as pollinators and beneficial insects.

## Time Horizons
- Short term: better moisture retention and surface protection
- Medium term: improved infiltration and microbial activity
- Long term: increased soil carbon, better structure, and more resilient biodiversity

## Useful Recommendation Patterns
- Low SOC + monoculture + low rainfall → suggest cover crops, residue retention, reduced tillage
- Low pH + poor growth → suggest pH testing and corrective amendments
- Compaction + poor infiltration → suggest reduced machinery pressure, organic matter addition, and diversified rooting crops
